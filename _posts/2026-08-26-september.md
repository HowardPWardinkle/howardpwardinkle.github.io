## Idea

Here's another study in black and white that breaks a run of three mice cards in a row. I had played around with pentagons and pentagrams in the past, but didn't have any luck with them. I think the main problem is the odd number of sides makes it more difficult since pop-ups like things square and symmetrical. This time I found something that turned out pretty well using two pentagrams, mirrored about a horizontal axis.

## Design

Here are the sketches for this.

![sketches](/images/2609/sketches.jpg.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Sketches*{: .image-caption}

1 was the first one with just two pentagrams. With 2, I played around with adding an additional V on the sides of each pentagram. The gold star is one of the attempts, but they all had problems with the pieces colliding when the card was closed. 3 is a full size one that I adding four triangle to each star. I also got very close on what the base would look like. That one used the sides of a pentagon to attach it to the base.

You may notice number 4's stars are bigger than the other full-size sketches. This was a mistake in my measurements, and when closing it, they stuck out -- a bit.

![Opps](/images/2609/too-big.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Oops!*{: .image-caption}

The fourth one is very close to the final mechanism. The upper star has the extra triangles and now is attached only on four points of the star with pieces of string holding each one down. This is a technique I've often seen in Chatani's books. I used it before as I mentioned in the [October 2024 card](/2024/09/23/october.html#design). The lower star is a pentagon attached on the sides. I used tape to experiment with the shape of the triangle's supports.

![supports](/images/2609/assembled.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Triangle supports*{: .image-caption}

The fifth one was a test printing and cutting the entire mechanism. I did find the points of the starts in the middle were too close and had to be wider. That's why the stars' point don't meet at the intersection of the lines on the base.

> The new {{ site.cameo5 }} does a much better job at the print and cut. I printed out all of the mechanisms and then had it cut out the stars and triangles. It even cut the slots for the triangle supports just about spot on. (The slots are 9mm x 0.5mm)

Figuring out the angle and length of the supports was the trickiest part of this card. I went through several iterations before finding ones that worked for both the upper and lower triangles that wouldn't collide when the card is closed. In the final version the "lower" triangles' supports will poke through the front as it closes. If I used a steep angle on the "lower" triangles they'd close earlier that the "upper" ones and avoid the collision, but they pointed nearly vertically, which I didn't like.

Here's the backs of the stars ready to assembly. Note this has tabs on all the supports, but I had to remove the lower ones after it assembled!

![back of triangle](/images/2609/back.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Backs of the stars*{: .image-caption}

To make the black pieces look better, I ran a marker along the edges.

![dark edges](/images/2609/dark-edges.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Dark edges*{: .image-caption}

Here's are the two stars assembled, with thread glued to four of their points ready to attach to the base. I poked six holes for the threads and carefully glued them on the back. Wherever I attached a thread, I glued on a piece of paper, just to make sure they don't come loose.

![ready to assemble](/images/2609/ready-assembly.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Ready to assemble*{: .image-caption}

Since the thread is glued on the back of the base, that means the cover will have three threads poking through it. That requires something to hide that, and since I used Howard Jr's artwork that is no problem this time. They do show on the back of the car.

The side view shows the stars attached just on the points. This gives it a much more interesting design since you can see under them.

![Side view](/images/2609/side-view.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Side view*{: .image-caption}

This *Study in Black and White* is the first is the second decade of studies. I think it turned out well, but removed another regular shape from the list of possiblities.
