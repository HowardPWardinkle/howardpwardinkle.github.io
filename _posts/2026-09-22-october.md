## Idea

My local bee club has a contest every September. Categories include different types of honey, wax, candles, etc. There is also a "Photography, art, and gadgets" category to which I decided to enter a pop-up card. My first idea was a bee flying into a flower, but the bee was pretty small. I ended up making one large bee. For inspiration, I looked at the Reinhart and Sabuda ["Beetles"](https://www.amazon.com/Young-Naturalists-Pop-Up-Handbook-Beetles/dp/0786805579) pop-up book.

## Design

The basic mechanism is a floating plane with the bee's legs as the supports. A parallel-fold sits on the plane to form the body of the bee. Only the the center (thorax) is attached to the plane. The bee is all one piece, but the head and rear (abdomen) are not connected to the plane. The idea with that is to allow them to form curved shapes as the card is opened.

Here are three sketches of the mechanism. The blue was the first, then orange to get the size right, and add the wings and antaennae. Finally, a full-size version, that was designed and cut on by the {{ site.cameo5 }}.

The full size sketch looked more like an ant than a bee since it wasn't furry. You can see I added a but of "fur" on the that version to see what it'd look like. I should have done another full-size sketch with the fur (that's foreshadowing). The front view of the sketch shows the floating plane with the triangle-shaped parallel-fold above it.

![sketch front](/images/2610/sketch-front.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Front view of the sketch*{: .image-caption}

I thought about just having the bee be white paper, but thought coloring it would be more interesting. Here's a photo of the two sides of the bee mostly colored in. It took quite a while to finish all of that up. I was a bit trepidatious when it came to assembling, since if something went wrong, I may have to redo the coloring. (And it's not a mouse, so the quality isn't as good as I'd like.)

![Colored](/images/2610/partially-colored.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Getting in colored in*{: .image-caption}

 Here's the back of the left side of the bee and all its parts. The paper attached to it will be attached to the other side. The two parts on the left make up the floating plane. The small T-shaped pieces are to connect the wings and antennae together.

![Assembly](/images/2610/assembly.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Ready for assembly*{: .image-caption}

Here I've attached all the pieces to the left side. The left wing and antenna are attached to the T-shaped pieces and the floating plane is attached to the thorax. I also colored more of the interior that would be visible.

![Assembing](/images/2610/assembled.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Assembling*{: .image-caption}

> The wings are printable transparency film, onto which I printed a design. To get smooth cuts, I used the print-and-cut functionality of the {{ site.cameo5 }}. I wondered how well it'd do with the clear film, and the first try didn't cut near the lines since it had trouble seeing the registration marks. I did some testing and found by putting paper under the registration marks, it did quite well.

> ![Registration](/images/2610/clear-registration.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthThird }}" }

I carefully attached the two sides together, making sure I didn't get glue where it shouldn't be. Here's a peek inside the bee so you can bee how the wings and antennae are attached.

![Inner](/images/2610/inner.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Inside the mechanism*{: .image-caption}

This is when I ran into my first problem. By doubling up the supports for the antennae, they were too thick and I couldn't close up the head. I hadn't glued the sides or bottom yet, so I removed the support, and replace it with a single-layerd one. Now I could glue the sides of the head together.

![Antennae](/images/2610/antennae.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Improved antennae*{: .image-caption}

I completed the cover and inside of the card with green pastels as the background, and printed the text. The pastels must be sealed, and before I've printed after it was sealed, but didn't get good results. I did a test print with different fixatives, and found the darkest colors were on the paper that had nothing on it. I fixed the pastels after the printing, and it worked fine.

![test](/images/2610/test.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Test of printing over fixatives*{: .image-caption}

Now that I had the base and the mechanism complete, it was just a simple matter of attaching the mechanism to the base. That seemed to go pretty well. I tried to really make sure the floating plane and the bee's feet were all aligned, that's when I noticed my next problem.

![Support](/images/2610/support.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Support misalignment*{: .image-caption}

Even though, I had the support cut and scored by the {{ site.cameo5 }}, something got misaligned during assembly, so I got a bit of a lean, which I could live with. The more annoying problem was the thorax and head didn't curve out, but curved in.

![Front views](/images/2610/support.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Sketch and final front views*{: .image-caption}

I think this was due to either me adding a piece to hold the wings up, or getting a bit too much glue on the top the the thorax and head. Mrs. Wardinkle didn't really notice, but I thought I could carefully separate the top edges a bit. Using a thin chisel, I got the thorax opened a bit, then...

![Oops](/images/2610/surgery-1.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Oops*{: .image-caption}

When trying to open the head, I broken the wing support!


Here are the two stars assembled, with thread glued to four of their points, ready to attach to the base. I poked six holes for the threads and carefully glued them on the back. Wherever I attached a thread, I glued on a piece of paper, just to make sure they don't come loose.

![ready to assemble](/images/2610/ready-assembly.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Ready to assemble*{: .image-caption}

Since the thread is glued on the back of the base in six places, the cover will have three threads poking through it. To hide that, I used Howard Jr's artwork. (They do show on the back of the card.)

The side view shows the stars attached just on the points. This gives it a much more interesting design since you can see under them.

![Side view](/images/2610/side-view.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Side view*{: .image-caption}

This *Study in Black and White* is the first of the second decade of studies. I think it turned out well, although it did remove another regular shape from the list of possibilities.
