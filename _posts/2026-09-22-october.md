## Idea

The bee club, which I am a member of, has a contest every September. Categories include honey, wax, and other products of the hive. In addition, there is a "Photography, art, and gadgets" category. I decided to enter a pop-up card in that category. My first idea was a bee flying into a flower, but the bee was pretty small. I ended up making one large bee. For inspiration, I looked at the Reinhart and Sabuda ["Beetles"](https://www.amazon.com/Young-Naturalists-Pop-Up-Handbook-Beetles/dp/0786805579) pop-up book.

## Design

The basic mechanism is a floating plane with the bee's legs as the supports. A parallel-fold sits on the plane to form the body of the bee. Only the the center (thorax) is attached to the plane. The bee is all one piece, but the head and rear (abdomen) are not connected to the plane. The idea with that is to allow them to form curved shaped as the card is opened.

![sketches](/images/2610/sketches.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Sketches*{: .image-caption}

Number 1 simply had two pentagrams (stars). With 2, I played around with adding additional Vs on the sides of each star. The gold star was one of the attempts, but all had problems with the pieces colliding when the card was closed. 3 was a full-size one that I added four triangles to each star. I also got very close on what the base would look like. That one used a pentagon with the top point cut out and used the sides of the pentagon to attach it to the base.

You may notice number 3's stars are bigger than the other full-size sketches. That was a mistake in my measurements, and when I closed it, they stuck out -- just a bit.

![Opps](/images/2610/too-big.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Oops!*{: .image-caption}

The fourth one is very close to the final mechanism. The upper star has extra triangles and is now attached only at four points, with pieces of string holding each one down. That is a technique I've often seen in Chatani's books. I used it before, as I mentioned in the [October 2024 card](/2024/09/23/october.html#design). The lower star is a pentagon attached to the sides. I used tape to experiment with the shape of the triangle's supports.

![supports](/images/2610/assembled.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Triangle supports*{: .image-caption}

The fifth was a test of printing and cutting of the entire mechanism. I found the points of the stars in the middle were too close and had to be wider. That's why the stars' points don't meet at the intersection of the lines on the base.

Figuring out the angle and length of the supports was the trickiest part of this card. I went through several iterations before finding ones that worked for both the upper and lower triangles that wouldn't collide when the card is closed. In the final version, the "lower" triangles' supports will slide through the star as it closes. If I used a steep angle on the "lower" triangles, they'd close earlier than the "upper" ones and avoid the collision, but they pointed nearly vertically, which I didn't like.

Here are the backs of the stars ready for assembly. Notice the tabs on all the supports. Even with the test sketches, in the final build I had to remove the "lower" tabs to allow sliding, which avoided collision.

![back of triangle](/images/2610/back.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Backs of the stars*{: .image-caption}

To make the black pieces look better, I ran a marker along the edges.

![dark edges](/images/2610/dark-edges.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Dark edges*{: .image-caption}

Here are the two stars assembled, with thread glued to four of their points, ready to attach to the base. I poked six holes for the threads and carefully glued them on the back. Wherever I attached a thread, I glued on a piece of paper, just to make sure they don't come loose.

![ready to assemble](/images/2610/ready-assembly.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Ready to assemble*{: .image-caption}

Since the thread is glued on the back of the base in six places, the cover will have three threads poking through it. To hide that, I used Howard Jr's artwork. (They do show on the back of the card.)

The side view shows the stars attached just on the points. This gives it a much more interesting design since you can see under them.

![Side view](/images/2610/side-view.jpg){: width="{{ site.imageWidthTwoThird }}" }
*Side view*{: .image-caption}

This *Study in Black and White* is the first of the second decade of studies. I think it turned out well, although it did remove another regular shape from the list of possibilities.
